Where can I get water?

Type your address. We'll show you the closest places to actually get water, who your official provider is, and whether that water has had safety violations on record.

    A town or crossroads works, it doesn't have to be an exact address. We don't store it, the lookup happens in your browser.

    How it works

    1. You type an address

      It's matched to a location using the U.S. Census Bureau's free geocoder. Nothing is saved and nothing is sent to us.

    2. We check who serves it

      Your location is checked against Arizona's official water provider service-area boundaries, then against the EPA's record of drinking-water violations for that provider.

    3. We show you where to go

      For communities we've researched, you get real places to get water: standpipes and haulers, with hours and phone numbers.

    Why this exists

    On January 1, 2023, Scottsdale cut off the hauled water that hundreds of homes in Rio Verde Foothills depended on. Those residents had no municipal provider, no city to call, and no single place to find out what their options were.

    The information that would have helped them existed, but it was scattered: haulers advertised on their own, federal compliance data sat in a database nobody browses, and neighbors ended up building their own website because nothing else covered it.

    This site pulls those pieces into one answer. It starts with Rio Verde Foothills because that's where the need was clearest and where real places to get water could be verified by hand.

    Know a water source we're missing?

    No government agency keeps a list of haulers and standpipes. This directory is built by hand, which means it's incomplete by default. If you know a source that should be here, tell us.

    Your water provider

    ADWR Community Water System service areas

    Official service-area boundaries. If your address falls outside every boundary, you have no municipal provider, which is itself the answer.

    Safety violations

    EPA SDWIS via ECHO

    Federal drinking-water compliance records. A violation is not automatically a health emergency, and no violations on record does not guarantee water is safe today.

    Regulatory context

    ADWR AMA/INA, AAWS, GWSI

    Whether you're in a managed groundwater area, whether your subdivision proved a 100-year supply, and monitored wells nearby. Background, not a verdict on your water.
